H3 — Writer cautions for this section
The strictest in this set, because harness buyers verify before they order and a failed verification ends the relationship permanently.
1.  Name only certifications actually held, with scope. The current website names none. Do not imply AS9100, ISO 9001, NADCAP, IPC/WHMA-A-620 operator certification or any customer approval unless it exists and the certificate is in hand.
2.  A standard named as a sector requirement is not a claim to hold it. Sections 4 and 5 describe the standards that govern this work, which is factual and fine. Only this section may say what Jetsys works to. That line is clean in this draft — check it survives editing.
3.  Do not claim a harness shop that does not exist. A fabricated in-house capability is discovered at the first audit, and the discovery is permanent.
4.  Do not claim programmes, platforms or customers without written permission. Defence supply references are frequently restricted and always verified.
5.  Do not publish capacity figures, operator counts or lead times that cannot be met on the day a buyer calls.
6.  If no certifications are held yet, say what the current offer is. A page stating "we supply harness components and are building toward in-house manufacture" is stronger commercially than one that implies capability and collapses under a single question.
