```text
Two points under this table, because both are commonly misunderstood.
Several light types exist in both forms, and runway guard lights are the clearest example. A runway guard light may be a pair of elevated alternately flashing yellow lights, one either side of the taxiway, or a row of in-pavement lights across it. The two serve the same purpose and are generally not both installed at the same runway holding position — though it can be advantageous to supplement in-pavement units with elevated ones where visibility of the holding position needs reinforcing.
The light's function does not dictate the fixture type on its own. Pavement width, whether aircraft actually cross that line, the approach category and the aerodrome's own layout all feed into it. That is why fixture type is settled in the lighting design rather than chosen product by product, and why the "Occasionally" column above is not hedging — it is the real answer.
```

## Frangibility, fixture qualification and cost

The article scopes the three-inch frangible point to the applicable FAA installation details. The factor-of-cost claim is recorded here pending Jetsys supply data. [FAA AC 150/5340-30J](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/150-5340-30J.pdf) · [FAA AC 150/5345-46F](https://www.faa.gov/airports/resources/advisory_circulars/index.cfm/go/document.current/documentNumber/150_5345-46) · [FAA AC 150/5345-42K](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC-150-5345-42K-Light-Bases.pdf)

```text
The stem sits in a frangible coupling designed to shear or break away cleanly on impact, so that a strike damages the light rather than the aircraft. The frangible point is kept low — specified as no higher than three inches above grade — and some coupling designs build in a fluid barrier to keep rainwater out of the base below once the coupling has broken. On elevated runway guard lights, a tether is sometimes added so that a fixture whose column breaks under jet blast is not blown onto a neighbouring runway or taxiway.
That third name is the honest one, because these fixtures are not perfectly flush. A small maximum projection above the surface is permitted — a fraction of an inch — and the fixture is designed and installed to stay within it. [Verify — confirm the permitted projection against the applicable specification before quoting a figure. None is quoted here.]
An elevated fixture is a sealed light on a pole. An inset fixture is a sealed, load-bearing, thermally cycled pressure vessel that must also be an accurate optical instrument — and that is why it costs several times as much.
An inset fixture sitting in sun-heated pavement can be hit by cold rain within seconds. As the internal volume contracts, the pressure differential pulls air — and any water present — past an imperfect seal. Thermal shock is a specific qualification test for these fixtures, alongside exposure to high relative humidity including condensation, and the top assembly must be sealed. [Verify — confirm any temperature range or test figure against the current specification revision before quoting one.]

So that a strike damages the light rather than the aircraft. The coupling is designed to shear cleanly on impact, and the frangible point is kept low — no higher than three inches above grade. It is a regulated, tested requirement that applies to the mounting as much as to the fixture.
```
